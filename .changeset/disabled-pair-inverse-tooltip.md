A disabled pair, `Semantics.Disabled` (decision 197, GAP-218): `Semantics.Disabled.fill`, background/100 (#E3E4E8 in light, #17191C in dark), and `Semantics.Disabled.foreground`, foreground/400 (#5D626F, #A29D90). In CSS `--semantics-disabled-fill` and `--semantics-disabled-foreground`; in TypeScript `SemanticsDisabledFill` and `SemanticsDisabledForeground`; in Swift and Kotlin `semanticsDisabledFill` and `semanticsDisabledForeground`; in the Android resources `semantics_disabled_fill` and `semantics_disabled_foreground`. The foreground reads 4.80:1 on the fill in light and 6.51:1 in dark, and `tokens:contrast:check` holds the pair to 3:1 or more.

**A disabled Button is no longer drawn at half opacity.** `.kozmos-button` loses `disabled:opacity-50`, and a disabled Button is drawn from the pair in every variant and emotion, at full strength. Its label, its icon and its spinner take the foreground. A filled variant (`default`, `destructive`, `secondary`, `subtle`, or any emotion's fill) takes the fill under them. An `outline`, `ghost` or `link` Button gains no fill: it keeps the page under it, where the foreground reads 6.10:1 and 7.76:1, and the outline's edge takes the foreground too. `glass` keeps its glass. A loading Button is disabled while it waits and is drawn the same. IconButton, FloatingActionButton, SplitButton (both halves) and MapControlButton take Button's props and are drawn the same; a disabled map control's surface takes the fill, on or off, as its surface is its fill. The cursor stays the default and the Button still takes no press. **This changes every disabled and loading Button's pixels.** An `aria-disabled` Button, as RouteSummary's step controls and ImageCropper's zoom are, keeps its focus and its own look, unchanged.

An inverse surface, `Semantics.Inverse` (decision 192, GAP-210): `Semantics.Inverse.surface`, foreground/200 (#2E3138 in light, #D1CEC7 in dark), and `Semantics.Inverse.onSurface`, background/0 (white, black), 13.0:1 and 13.4:1. In CSS `--semantics-inverse-surface` and `--semantics-inverse-on-surface`; in TypeScript `SemanticsInverseSurface` and `SemanticsInverseOnSurface`; in Swift and Kotlin `semanticsInverseSurface` and `semanticsInverseOnsurface`; in the Android resources `semantics_inverse_surface` and `semantics_inverse_on_surface`. The stylesheet adds the `inverse` colour role (`bg-inverse`, `text-inverse-foreground`, `border-inverse`, `fill-inverse`).

`TooltipContent` takes `tone="inverse"`: the inverse surface under its words, its border and its arrow in the surface's colour, the same size as the default. In light it is exactly Pointr Cloud's tooltip, #2E3138 under white. The default tone, the popover surface with its border, is unchanged.
