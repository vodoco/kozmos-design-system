# ManoeuvreCard

The current manoeuvre, floating over the map during navigation: its arrow, the instruction, how far and how long, and a grab bar that opens the full itinerary in its place. The card owns the toggle and what assistive technology hears of it; the itinerary it opens into is the caller's, so the card never decides what a route is made of.

Open, the card is as tall as the itinerary up to a cap (320 by default), past which the itinerary scrolls: a long route must not cover the map. The closed card is one button that reads the instruction, then the detail; the grab bar is silent then, and becomes the way to close the card once it is open.

On `surface="glass"` the detail, muted elsewhere, takes the foreground colour, and so do the itinerary's captions and origin inside it, so they read at 4.5:1 over any map (decision 48). Muted over a saturated map they read as low as 2.5:1 on iOS and Android and 3.6:1 on the web. On iOS and Android the card tells what it holds which surface it is on (`kozmosSurfaceStyle`, `LocalKozmosSurfaceStyle`).

## The whole instruction

The card shows the whole instruction, and grows with it. Two lines cut ordinary instructions short: "Take the escalator near Fountain Court up to…" loses the level, and German "Biegen Sie bei Marlow Apotheke auf der linke…" loses the turn itself, "rechts ab" (GAP-094). Examples/Navigation's phone stories show English, German and Japanese instructions whole on a 390 phone, and the German itinerary scrolling.

- **A limit, if a product wants one:** `instructionLines` on every platform (SwiftUI's `lineLimit`, Compose's `maxLines`). The instruction ends in an ellipsis at that many lines. Unset, or under one line, the whole instruction shows. Assistive technology hears the whole instruction either way.
- **The itinerary keeps its own cap:** `maxItineraryHeight`.
- **Before:** Kozmos 0.5 and earlier cut the instruction at two lines. The whole instruction is the default now, as MAP-111's US1-EC8 asks; `instructionLines={2}` draws what they drew.

## Reaching the itinerary

Past its cap the itinerary scrolls, and every step stays reachable (GAP-100):

- **On the web** the box that scrolls is a stop of its own in the tab order: a group named after the itinerary it holds, `itineraryLabel` ("Itinerary" until the product passes its own words, the same as `Itinerary`'s `label`). Tab reaches it, Page Down, Space and End scroll it, and it draws the focus ring. It is a group, not a second landmark: the `Itinerary` inside is that already. Before, it could not take focus (axe's `scrollable-region-focusable`, serious), so a keyboard could not reach the steps past the cap.
- **VoiceOver** reaches every step: each is an element, and the itinerary scrolls to the one VoiceOver moves to. Read from outside the app with XCUITest, all nine steps of the German example are there, and the destination below the cap scrolls into view.
- **TalkBack** reaches every step: each is a node of its own, and the list offers TalkBack the scroll that brings the ones below the cap into view.

## Focus through the disclosure

Opening takes the instruction away, and closing hides the grab bar. Focus that was on the part that went moves to the part in its place; focus anywhere else stays where it is, whether a key, a pointer or the product opened or closed the card. No timers.

- **On the web:** opening from the instruction puts focus on the scrolling itinerary; closing from Hide, or from inside the itinerary, puts it back on the instruction. The closed, silent grab bar never takes focus, even from a pointer. A pointer that focuses what it presses (Chromium) is followed; one that does not (WebKit, Firefox on macOS) moves nothing. Before, focus fell to the page on opening, and closing left it on the grab bar, hidden from assistive technology.
- **On iOS:** VoiceOver's focus follows the same rule (`@AccessibilityFocusState`, as FloorSelector's column uses it): from the instruction to the itinerary as the card opens, from Hide back to the instruction as it closes. VoiceOver does not run in the Simulator, so the rule is tested and the move itself wants a device.
- **On Android:** input focus follows the rule, and TalkBack with it, since Compose reports the move (`TYPE_VIEW_FOCUSED`). Opening from the instruction puts focus on Hide, the control that took its place: a focusable box around the steps would be a silent stop for TalkBack. The closed grab bar is no longer a keyboard stop. A tap moves no focus.
