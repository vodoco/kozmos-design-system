# MapOverlay

A positioned surface that floats product chrome above the map canvas.

## Usage

Use it for anything that sits over the map rather than beside it:

- A search bar anchored to the top of the canvas
- A route summary along the bottom edge
- Floating controls and status chips

Do not render map content inside it. The canvas is renderer output; an overlay
is the app's own UI layered on top.

Pass `collisionInsets` from the map adapter. Overlays that ignore them end up
covering the renderer's attribution, which is usually a licensing requirement,
not a cosmetic preference.

`width` is a t-shirt scale on web. On native the host lays the overlay out with
frame and modifier sizing instead, which is recorded as intentional in the
variant analyzer rather than counted as a parity gap.

What the overlay holds scrolls when it is taller than the map leaves room for.
The overlay keeps the reach of the shadows it holds clear around them — the
floating elevation's and the map controls' own, the larger of the two on each
side: 32px above and beside, 48px below — so a control's shadow and its focus
ring are drawn whole at the overlay's edges; while it scrolls, only what has
scrolled out of view is cut. The room is read from the elevation tokens when the
package is built, and it takes no space from the layout: the content sits where
it would without it.

The room is for the shadows, not for presses (decision 46, which replaces
decision 31's cost). Only what the overlay holds takes a press. A press or a
drag in the room, or in the space between the overlay's items, reaches the map
beneath, so the map pans from right beside a control. It still scrolls: a wheel
or a touch over what it holds scrolls the overlay, and so does focus moving
into it. Where a platform draws a scrollbar that takes room of its own, the bar
sits in the room and passes presses to the map like the rest of it.

## Accessibility

- The overlay adds no landmark of its own; its content keeps its own reading
  order and roles.
- Contrast comes from the surface token, not from the basemap underneath, so
  legibility does not depend on what the map happens to be showing.
- Respect `collisionInsets` so overlays never cover attribution or the
  renderer's own controls.
