# POIResultCard

Product result pattern that shares a stable POI ID and result index with its
map marker. Selection is controlled through `result.selected`; unavailable
results remain readable but cannot invoke `onSelect`.

The selected result shows the actions `result.actions` carries, and a press
reaches `onAction` with the action and the POI's ID. `POIResultList` hands its
`onAction` to every card, on all three platforms: see its page. A card given actions
but no `onAction` draws them disabled on every platform. Disabled actions do not
invoke a callback or report an action analytics event. Adding or removing the handler
updates their availability; an action explicitly marked disabled remains disabled.

Result actions have a **44-unit minimum height and width** on web, iOS and Android
(GAP-056), including disabled actions. The control grows for larger text rather
than imposing a fixed-height clip. iOS includes the padding inside the button's
hit area; Android retains Material's larger touch-target policy. The Figma importer
draws 44px action buttons. This is the SDK's sizing contract, not a claim that every
smaller control necessarily fails WCAG, or that physical-device acceptance is complete.

## Requested staff language

`result.languageNotListed === true` draws a quiet, wrapping **Language not listed**
note below the select row. Pass `languageNotListedLabel` to localize it or name the
requested language. Lists and groups forward that label; native cards and lists
use the same field and label. The note is also announced with the select row,
even when a custom `selectionLabel` replaces its name. On web it is an accessible
description, preserving the custom name and any unavailable explanation.

Missing data is unknown. `false` merely suppresses this note: neither value proves
that staff speak a language. The host supplies explicit evidence, may group results
using it, and owns ranking and the requested language. `match`, `nameLanguage`, UI
translation fallback and device locale do not infer this field. Selection, actions
and availability remain independent. Existing callers need no migration.

Swift's `selecting` copy preserves the field and the existing `summary`; previously
that copy dropped `summary`. Native source support is not native registry delivery
or physical VoiceOver/TalkBack acceptance.

## The name's language and the summary's

A result's name is shown exactly as authored, and its `summary` is written by the model in
the query's language: a visitor who asks in Spanish on an English device reads a Spanish
summary among English labels (MAP-474 US2-EC1). `result.nameLanguage` and
`result.summaryLanguage` say which language each is in, as BCP 47 tags, set only when it
differs from the interface language. The card sets each as the `lang` of that text's own
span, never the card's, so a screen reader changes voice for those words alone (WCAG 3.1.2;
GAP-004, GAP-125). With no tag, or an empty one, there is no `lang` attribute.
`POIResultList` and `POIResultGroup` draw their results through the card, so listed and
grouped rows keep both tags.

SwiftUI and Compose draw the summary as the web does, muted and two lines at most, and read
it after the location. When either tag is set, VoiceOver hears the select row through a UIKit
element whose label carries each tagged range's speech language: the same words, state and
action as without tags, each phrase in its own voice. TalkBack is given the row's words as its
text, tagged or not, never as a description, with a `LocaleSpan` over each tagged range; the
texts drawn inside the row are left out, so each phrase is heard once. A `selectionLabel` replaces the whole
name, so the host words it in one language. Physical VoiceOver and TalkBack review remains
separate.

## Default SDK presentation and migration

`presentationStyle="sdk"` is now the default on React, SwiftUI and Compose.
Selection uses the same pale neutral surface for standalone and grouped rows,
not a blue card outline. Keyboard focus remains a separate visible indicator.
Pointer hover is 5% darker than the approved prior hover fill: `#EEEFF0` in light
mode; selected cards retain `#F9FAFB`, even while hovered. Both colors have themed
`Semantics.Result` tokens; no touch-only hover state is introduced.

With `numbered`, the corner tab reads **1234 ★ Featured**, **1234 Alternative** or
**1234**. The star is the Featured separator, with no dash or dot. Numbering remains
off unless requested, and uses the supplied `resultIndex` even for Featured—never a
new list position. The product still owns sprite numbering and selected map layers.

Tabs have 10-unit side padding, 1-unit vertical padding and a 20-unit minimum height.
Their top-start and bottom-end radii are both 16. In a group, only the first row shares
the outer rounded top-start; subsequent rows have square starts and retain the rounded
bottom-end tab. Labels participate in layout and may grow; names wrap instead of being
forced onto one line. `navigate` actions gain a decorative outlined navigation icon;
their localized label and callbacks do not change.

**Migration:** no data or handler signature change is required. Expect changed card heights,
wrapping names, accessible names that include the combined tab once, and updated visual
baselines. Consumers relying on the former appearance can temporarily set
`presentationStyle="legacy"`, Swift `.legacy`, or Compose
`KozmosPOIResultPresentationStyle.Legacy` on a card/list/group. Lists forward this setting.
Custom `selectionLabel` still replaces the whole accessible name. White-label consumers
may override the two result surface tokens; verify their text contrast and focus treatment.
Physical screen-reader review, Figma adoption and SDK sprite integration remain separate.

## Legacy presentation: one tab, with precedence

The following precedence applies only to explicit `presentationStyle="legacy"`.

A card draws at most one tab inside its top-start corner (the right in a right-to-left language).
It shares the card's outer curve and edge; only its inner corner rounds. The name begins below
the tab, never underneath it. What it says decides how it looks, and the first that applies wins:

1. **Featured** (`result.featured`): the SDK's bright amber under dark words, with a star, and the
   card's edge in the same amber, selected or not (the ring says which card is selected). It is the
   alert fill pair, `--semantics-emotion-alert-fill` and `--semantics-emotion-alert-on-fill`, which
   stays bright in both themes. Featured is set in the CMS and read beyond the card: a featured
   result's pin shows its logo on the map.
2. **A number** (`numbered`, off unless the product turns it on): the result's `resultIndex`, the
   number its pin shows. At rest the tab is quiet, outlined on the card's own fill, and the card
   keeps its grey edge, so a number never reads as the selected card. Selected, the tab fills with
   the primary colour as the card's edge turns primary, the way the selected pin stands out from
   the rest.
3. **A badge** (`result.badge`): why the result is in this list, such as "Alternative". It is quiet:
   a neutral tab with no star, on the card's grey edge, so it is never taken for Featured (GAP-054).

So a featured result shows Featured and no number, and a numbered result shows its number rather
than its badge, so the list's numbers match the pins.

- **The numbers are the product's.** The card draws the `resultIndex` it is given and never
  renumbers. `resultIndex` counts from 1: it is the number the result's map marker shows, and the
  position analytics reports. Turn numbering on for a list whose pins are numbered, with
  `POIResultList`'s `numbered`; quick access is the case, where a category chosen in the browse
  grid lists its places and the map pins them. Number the results that are not featured 1, 2, 3 in
  pin order, since a featured result's pin shows its logo.
- **What is heard.** The number's tab is decorative. The number leads the result's accessible name
  instead, "2, Burger King …", so it is heard once, with the result it numbers, and no words are
  added. A `selectionLabel` replaces that whole name, so a product that passes one says the number
  in its own words. The Featured and badge tabs are read as text, as they always were.
- **In a group.** A `POIResultGroup` row has no edge of its own to hang a tab from, so a numbered
  row draws its number before its name, painted as the tab is.
- **The colours** are owned rules, so they hold without `@scope`, and each tab's words read at
  4.5:1 or more on its fill in both themes: Featured 10.56:1 light and 13.14:1 dark; the number
  6.10:1 and 7.76:1 at rest, 6.24:1 and 6.17:1 selected; the badge 4.80:1 and 6.51:1. iOS and
  Android draw the same pairs.

## A walk shown as a band

A result list shows the walk to a place as a band: **Nearby** under a minute, then **1–2 min**,
**2–5 min**, **5–10 min** and **More than 10 min** (decision 50). The product passes the walking
time it already has, with the band Kozmos's rule gives it, `travelTimeBand(durationSeconds)` from
`@kozmos-ds/product-contracts`, and the card draws the band's words. Every product that uses the
rule draws the edges in the same place.

- **The edges.** Each band keeps its upper edge: a place exactly 2 minutes away reads 1–2 min, and
  one 2 minutes 1 second away reads 2–5 min; exactly 5 and 10 minutes read 2–5 and 5–10. Past the
  first minute that is the walk rounded up to whole minutes. A length below zero, or one that is not
  a finite number, has no band, and the card shows the exact minutes.
- **The tone.** Nearby is drawn in the success colour, which reads at 4.5:1 or more on the card in
  both themes (7.1:1 light, 17.7:1 dark); the other bands are the card's text colour. The word
  itself says Nearby, so the tone is never carried by colour alone.
- **The words** are English until the product passes its own as `travelTimeBandLabels`, one band or
  all five; `POIResultList` and `POIResultGroup` hand them to every result. The same name on iOS
  and Android.
- **The details card** keeps the exact minutes. The estimate carries both: `band` for the list and
  `durationLabel` for `POIDetailPanel`, which ignores the band.
- **Without a band** a result shows `durationLabel`, as it always has.
