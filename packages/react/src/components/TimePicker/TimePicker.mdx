# TimePicker

A time field: the browser's own, with its segmented typing in the reader's clock, and a button at
its end that opens a list of times (decision 104), Combobox's list as Figma's Open variant
(`444:11455`) draws it. The value is an `HH:MM` string, as `<input type="time">`'s is, through the
input's own `value`, `defaultValue` and `onChange`; a time chosen from the list is typed into the
field, so `onChange` and a form's change listeners hear it as typing.

External `aria-describedby` IDs merge with helper/error guidance, and an explicit component error
remains invalid. The owned field layout follows module direction and exposes a focus indicator for
native segments, including engines that do not match them with `:focus-visible`. The host owns any
time-zone or scheduling rules.

## The list of times

The button, and Alt+Down Arrow or F4 in the field, open the list under the field, as wide as it, on
the field's time; the list takes focus, and the arrows, Home and End move in it. A time chosen by
Enter, Space or the pointer is typed into the field; that or Escape closes the list and returns focus
to the button. It works in every engine: Chromium's own clock button gives way to Kozmos's, and
Safari and Firefox draw none, so for the first time they offer a list.

The times run from `min` to `max` (the input's own, `00:00` to `23:59` by default) every `step`
seconds, or every 30 minutes when `step` asks for less than a minute or nothing. They read in
`locale`'s own short time (`09:30`, `9:30 AM`), by default the document's language, and `hourCycle`
sets 12 or 24 hours; the typed field keeps the browser's clock. `chooseTimeLabel` names the button
and, with no `label`, the list (`"Choose time"` by default). `open` with `onOpenChange` controls it,
and `defaultOpen` opens it at first; Storybook's `Open` story draws it open. Disabled or read only,
the button cannot open it.

## In Safari

`className` lands on the field. A utility class passed there styles it in Chrome, Edge,
Firefox and Safari 26.4, and is ignored in Safari 26.0 to 26.3, where the field keeps its
own look: those versions apply no `@scope` rule to an `<input>` (GAP-174), and Kozmos's
utilities are scoped. Use the props, the provider's `tokens`, an inline `style` or your
own CSS, and `wrapperClassName` for the layout around it. Guides › Platform support ›
Fields in Safari has the detail.
