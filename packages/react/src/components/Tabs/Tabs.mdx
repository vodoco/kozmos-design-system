# Tabs

Switches between related content panels within the same interface.
Compose TabsList, TabsTrigger and TabsContent with matching values; the Radix-backed root supports controlled and uncontrolled selection.

Every platform draws the same tabs: a muted track (background/100), and the selected tab as a raised segment inside it, on background/0 with foreground/0 words, while the other tabs' words are foreground/400. Tabs carry no theme colour (decision 65).

## An icon over the words

`TabsTrigger` takes an `icon`: it is drawn 20px, 4px above the tab's words and in their colour, and the list grows to hold it, 64 where a list of words is 40 (GAP-152). Once one tab in a list has an icon, every tab in it takes that height, so the raised segment fills the track whichever tab is active, and a tab of words alone centres them. The icon is drawn only; the tab is named by its words. An `asChild` tab stays the caller's element, with the icon inside it. A height class you give `TabsList` still wins. In right to left the stack is the same, and the tabs run from the right.

SwiftUI's `KozmosTabsTrigger(value:title:selection:icon:)` and Compose's `KozmosTabsTrigger(..., icon = { ... })` draw the same stack, and there too every tab in a list with an icon takes the stacked height. Only tabs inside the list do: a SwiftUI trigger standing alone keeps its own height. Compose's `KozmosTabsList` sizes its tabs by their intrinsic height, so put only `KozmosTabsTrigger`s in it: a composable placed straight in the list that cannot be measured that way, a `SubcomposeLayout` such as `BoxWithConstraints`, a lazy list or `SubcomposeAsyncImage`, throws there. A trigger's `icon` may be any of these, because it is drawn in a fixed 20dp box.

## When the tabs don't fit

A list wider than the space it is given scrolls sideways inside its own track (GAP-254, decision 182). The track stays the width of the space, so the page never widens; each tab keeps its natural width, its words never cut short or wrapped. No scroll bar is drawn, on any platform; touch, a trackpad and a wheel scroll it. The selected tab is in view when the list is first shown (Structural below, without scrolling) and is brought into view, by the least distance, whenever the selection moves; a tab the keyboard focuses is brought into view too. The move is animated unless motion is reduced. Right to left the list starts at the right and scrolls the other way. It is still one tablist of tabs; the track adds no role and no tab stop.

A list that fits is drawn exactly as before. Only a list that overflows becomes a scroll container, because a scroll container clips what is drawn past its edge: the track's 4px padding is inside it, so the raised segment, its shadow and the focus ring are drawn in the track, and only the shadow's faintest tail, which reaches 2px below the track, is cut at its edge. A list that fits keeps that tail.

SwiftUI's `KozmosTabsList` lays out today's row while its tabs' natural widths fit, and otherwise a horizontal `ScrollView` with no indicators, the track as its background and clip, scrolling the selected tab into view with a `ScrollViewReader`. Compose's `KozmosTabsList` lays its tabs out as a `Row` would while they fit, weights and all, and otherwise at their natural widths in a `horizontalScroll`, scrolled to the selected tab, animated unless the system has removed animations. Neither needs anything from you.
