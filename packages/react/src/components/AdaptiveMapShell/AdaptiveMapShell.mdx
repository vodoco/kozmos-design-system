# AdaptiveMapShell

Map-engine-neutral layout driven by its **container**, not the browser viewport.
Give the shell a definite height (or a bounded parent for its default `height: 100%`).
The old 448px minimum has been removed so a short landscape host can constrain it.

Auto presentation uses a side panel in a local region at least 720×160px, otherwise
a bottom panel. Override with `panelPresentation`; a bottom sheet rests at one of its `panelDetents` — collapsed (a fifth of the shell, or
the content's peek anchor, marked with `panelPeekAnchorProps`), medium (54 %) and large
(94 %) by default, resting at medium — dragged anywhere on it and snapping to the nearest;
its content scrolls only at the largest detent. `panelFraction` and `panelSizing="content"`
are single-detent shorthands. Read the resolved
panel bounds from `onLayoutChange`. `panelPlacement` is logical and follows inherited RTL direction.

`panelHeader` sits under the sheet's grip and above the panel's content, and does not
scroll with it, so a search field or a chosen category stays put while the results under
it scroll. Every detent counts it: a sheet fitted to its content includes it, and a
collapsed sheet is always tall enough to show all of it (a header that fits leaves the
collapsed detent where it was; a taller one raises it). A peek anchor, in the header or the
content, still decides where a collapsed sheet rests. A vertical drag on it always
moves the sheet, however far the content has scrolled; a sideways one stays with the
header, for a row of chips that scrolls. Under a grab handle it keeps its first control
clear of the handle's target (WCAG 2.5.8). In a side panel it is the panel's first row.

The panel tells its content how much space it already leaves above it, so a part that brings its
own top padding can top it up rather than add to it:

- `--kozmos-panel-inset-top` is the empty space above the content: the grip's row on a sheet that
  draws one, `1rem` in a side panel, and `0px` with no grip or under a `panelHeader`.
- `--kozmos-panel-clearance-top` is how far the content's first control must still sit below that
  so the grip's 16px target keeps its WCAG 2.5.8 spacing: 4px under a grip, `0px` elsewhere. A
  panel header keeps the same clearance itself.

`POIDetailPanel` in its `sheet` and `panel` presentations uses both, so its close button sits as far
from the panel's top as from its side (GAP-083).

iOS and Android take the same header: `panelHeader:` on `KozmosAdaptiveMapShell` in
SwiftUI, and `panelHeader =` in Compose. On iOS, put the content that scrolls in a
`KozmosPanelScrollView`, so the sheet can take a drag from it.

On iOS and Android the shell tells its panel's content how much space it already leaves
above it, so a part that brings its own top padding tops it up rather than adding to it
(GAP-083): `kozmosPanelInsetTop` and `kozmosPanelClearanceTop` in the SwiftUI environment,
`LocalKozmosPanelInsetTop` and `LocalKozmosPanelClearanceTop` in Compose. Under a grab
handle they are its 16-point row and 4 points of clearance, so the handle's target keeps
its WCAG 2.5.8 spacing; with no handle (a single detent), under a `panelHeader`, and in a
side panel, which starts its content at its top edge, both are zero. `KozmosPOIDetailPanel`
in its sheet and panel presentations uses both, so its close button sits as far from the
panel's top as from its side; a card placed lower in the panel than its top should use the
inline presentation, which keeps its own padding.

`usableRegions` accepts host-provided hinge-free rectangles in shell-local CSS pixels.
Auto mode can separate map and panel across those regions without remounting either.
This is a geometry input, not automatic foldable-device detection.

The `map` slot must be a real supported renderer; this component never draws substitute
map geometry. `onLayoutChange` reports shell-local renderer/panel bounds and occlusions.
Resolved padding is **map-local**, exposed through `onCollisionInsetsChange` and
`--kozmos-map-inset-*`. The SDK adapter applies it to the camera deliberately.

`safeAreaInsets` reserves edge space (including keyboard overlap supplied by the host);
do not subtract a keyboard again if it already resized the host. See
`docs/adaptive-map-layout.md` for the full coordinate and fallback contract.
