# AdaptiveMapShell

Map-engine-neutral layout driven by its **container**, not the browser viewport.
Give the shell a definite height (or a bounded parent for its default `height: 100%`).
The old 448px minimum has been removed so a short landscape host can constrain it.

Auto presentation uses a side panel in a local region at least 720×160px, otherwise
a bottom panel. Override with `panelPresentation`; a bottom sheet rests at one of its `panelDetents` — collapsed (a fifth of the shell, or
the content's peek anchor, marked with `panelPeekAnchorProps`), medium (54 %) and large
(94 %) by default, resting at medium — dragged anywhere on it and snapping to the nearest;
its content scrolls only at the largest detent. `panelFraction` and `panelSizing="content"`
are single-detent shorthands. Read the resolved
panel bounds from `onLayoutChange`. `panelPlacement` is logical and follows inherited RTL direction.

`panelHeader` sits under the sheet's grip and above the panel's content, and does not
scroll with it, so a search field or a chosen category stays put while the results under
it scroll. Every detent counts it: a sheet fitted to its content includes it, and a
collapsed sheet is always tall enough to show all of it (a header that fits leaves the
collapsed detent where it was; a taller one raises it). A peek anchor, in the header or the
content, still decides where a collapsed sheet rests. A vertical drag on it always
moves the sheet, however far the content has scrolled; a sideways one stays with the
header, for a row of chips that scrolls. Under a grab handle it keeps its first control
clear of the handle's target (WCAG 2.5.8). In a side panel it is the panel's first row.

The panel tells its content how much space it already leaves above it, so a part that brings its
own top padding can top it up rather than add to it:

- `--kozmos-panel-inset-top` is the empty space above the content: the grip's row on a sheet that
  draws one, `1rem` in a side panel, and `0px` with no grip or under a `panelHeader`.
- `--kozmos-panel-clearance-top` is how far the content's first control must still sit below that
  so the grip's 16px target keeps its WCAG 2.5.8 spacing: 4px under a grip, `0px` elsewhere. A
  panel header keeps the same clearance itself.

Every part at the top of the panel keeps the grip's clearance, so no first control sits inside the
grip's 24px circle:

- `POIDetailPanel` in its `sheet` presentation, which paints no surface of its own,
  `BrowseCategoriesPanel`, whose first row is its search row (or its tiles when it has none), and
  `RoutePreviewPanel`, whose first row is its destination, top their 16px up to what the panel
  leaves, `max(clearance, 16px − inset)`. The close button, the search field and the destination
  sit as far from the panel's top as from their side, and 4px further under a grip (GAP-083 and
  decision 14; the search field and "To" sat 33px down and 17px in).
- `POIResultList` brings no top padding of its own, so it keeps only the clearance. Under a grip
  its first result used to sit inside the grip's circle.

The two values describe the panel's top. A part you place under a row of your own is not at the
top: put that row in `panelHeader`, which keeps the grip clear itself, or set both properties to
`0px` on what follows the row.

The shell's panel, sheet or side, has one surface, solid or glass, and a part it hosts paints no
fill of its own (decision 43). The panel sets `--kozmos-panel-part-fill` to `transparent` on its
content and its header. `BrowseCategoriesPanel` and `RoutePreviewPanel`, which fill their box with
the background colour when they stand alone, paint nothing there, so a glass panel shows through
them. On a solid panel they look as they did, since its fill is that same colour. `POIResultList`
and `POIDetailPanel`'s `sheet` presentation paint no fill anywhere, and cards keep theirs: each
result, and a `panel` or `inline` details card.

On glass, text that is muted elsewhere takes the foreground colour, so it reads at 4.5:1 over any
map (decision 48); the glass itself is unchanged. The glass surface says so: `.kozmos-surface-glass`
sets `--kozmos-surface-muted-foreground` to the foreground colour, and the solid surface resets it.
A hosted part's muted text that sits on the panel reads the property: the route preview's "To"
and count of options, the details card's level line, hours, prices, notes, section headings and
gallery position in its `sheet` presentation, and the empty states of the browser, the route
preview and the result list. Over a saturated map it read about 3.7:1 muted; it now reads 9.7:1 or
more. Muted text in a card of its own, a result, a route option or a `panel` or `inline` details
card, stays muted: it sits on the card.

iOS and Android take the same header: `panelHeader:` on `KozmosAdaptiveMapShell` in
SwiftUI, and `panelHeader =` in Compose. On iOS, put the content that scrolls in a
`KozmosPanelScrollView`, so the sheet can take a drag from it.

On iOS and Android the shell tells its panel's content how much space it already leaves
above it, so a part that brings its own top padding tops it up rather than adding to it
(GAP-083): `kozmosPanelInsetTop` and `kozmosPanelClearanceTop` in the SwiftUI environment,
`LocalKozmosPanelInsetTop` and `LocalKozmosPanelClearanceTop` in Compose. Under a grab
handle they are its 16-point row and 4 points of clearance, so the handle's target keeps
its WCAG 2.5.8 spacing; with no handle (a single detent), under a `panelHeader`, and in a
side panel, which starts its content at its top edge, both are zero. `KozmosPOIDetailPanel`
in its sheet presentation, which paints no surface of its own, uses both, so its close
button sits as far from the panel's top as from its side. The panel and inline presentations
draw their own bordered card and keep their padding inside it: the space the panel leaves
lies outside that border. `KozmosBrowseCategoriesPanel` uses both for its first row too: in
its sheet presentation on iOS (the panel presentation paints its own surface and keeps its
16), and always on Android, where it paints no surface. `KozmosRoutePreviewPanel` uses both
for its destination row on both platforms. Under the grab handle the
`panelHeader` itself starts 4 points (4dp) below the handle's row, as the web's does. For a
part you place under a row of your own, set both values to zero: `.environment(\.kozmosPanelInsetTop, 0)`
and `.environment(\.kozmosPanelClearanceTop, 0)` in SwiftUI, and `0.dp` for both
CompositionLocals in Compose.

The shell also tells what it hosts which surface it sits on, `kozmosPanelSurface` in the
SwiftUI environment and `LocalKozmosPanelSurface` in Compose (solid or glass, nil outside a
shell), so a part paints no fill of its own there (decision 43). `KozmosRoutePreviewPanel`
paints none in the panel on both platforms; standing alone it keeps its background colour.
The category browser paints none in its iOS sheet presentation and none at all on Android,
and the details card's sheet presentation none on either. On iOS the route preview's square
fill also covered the panel's rounded top corners; they show the map again. The same value
says when their muted text takes the foreground colour: on a glass panel (decision 48), for
the route preview's "To" and count of options and the details card's muted lines in its
sheet presentation, which tells what it holds that a bordered card is a surface of its own.

`usableRegions` accepts host-provided hinge-free rectangles in shell-local CSS pixels.
Auto mode can separate map and panel across those regions without remounting either.
This is a geometry input, not automatic foldable-device detection.

The `map` slot must be a real supported renderer; this component never draws substitute
map geometry. `onLayoutChange` reports shell-local renderer/panel bounds and occlusions.
Resolved padding is **map-local**, exposed through `onCollisionInsetsChange` and
`--kozmos-map-inset-*`. The SDK adapter applies it to the camera deliberately.

`safeAreaInsets` reserves edge space (including keyboard overlap supplied by the host);
do not subtract a keyboard again if it already resized the host. See
`docs/adaptive-map-layout.md` for the full coordinate and fallback contract.
