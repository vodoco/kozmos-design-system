# AICompanionPanel

The assistant surface. It covers the frame and leaves the search sheet
untouched beneath, so closing it returns the visitor to the search they left.

`onClose` is optional: a host app may turn the assistant off, and the panel
has to tolerate `AISearchButton` being absent. Escape closes it when there is
something to close — a surface covering the frame that can only be dismissed
by clicking its × is a trap for anyone not using a pointer. It closes this
panel only, and goes no further.

A product's own `onKeyDown` runs first, and Escape still closes the panel
after it. An Escape already handled is left alone: one the product's handler
keeps with `event.preventDefault()`, or one a part inside has used to dismiss
something of its own, a field clearing itself or a list of suggestions
closing, and marked the same way.

Put the thread and its input inside, in that order. `banner` sits above the
thread, for a `Notice` or an offline `EmptyState`.

## Finding it, and focus

The panel is a **region** named by its title, and the title is a heading.
`titleLevel` — 2 by default — follows whatever heading sits above the panel;
it looks the same at every level.

It moves focus itself, when the visitor opens and closes it. Keep the panel
mounted and pass `open`; closed, it draws nothing.

- When `open` turns **true**, the panel takes focus: a screen reader
  announces it, and Escape works at once. Focus left on the button beneath
  would be on something the visitor can no longer see.
- When `open` turns **false**, or the panel unmounts, it hands focus back to
  whatever had it when it opened — usually `AISearchButton`, which the panel
  covered rather than replaced.
- A panel that is **open as it mounts** — on screen from the start, with
  `open` left out or true from the first render — was opened by nobody, so it
  leaves focus where the page put it. Closed and opened again, it takes focus
  like any other open.

A product that mounts the panel to open it is showing it from the start, as
far as the panel can tell, and focus stays on the button beneath. Keep it
mounted and turn `open` on instead. Place the panel itself, through its
`className`: a wrapper kept mounted around it would stay over the sheet,
empty, once it closes.

```tsx
const [open, setOpen] = useState(false);

<>
  <AISearchButton onClick={() => setOpen(true)} />
  <AICompanionPanel
    className="absolute inset-0"
    open={open}
    onClose={() => setOpen(false)}
  >
    <AIMessageList>{turns}</AIMessageList>
    <AIInputBar onSubmit={ask} onValueChange={setValue} value={value} />
  </AICompanionPanel>
</>;
```

Either move can be redirected with the two callbacks Kozmos's `Dialog` and
`Drawer` already take. Each is called first; `event.preventDefault()` and put
focus where it should go yourself — the field, for a product that wants the
keyboard up at once:

```tsx
const field = useRef<HTMLInputElement>(null);

<AICompanionPanel
  open={open}
  onClose={() => setOpen(false)}
  onOpenAutoFocus={(event) => {
    event.preventDefault();
    field.current?.focus();
  }}
>
  <AIMessageList>{turns}</AIMessageList>
  <AIInputBar
    inputRef={field}
    onSubmit={ask}
    onValueChange={setValue}
    value={value}
  />
</AICompanionPanel>;
```

`onOpenAutoFocus` is called only when `open` turns true, never for a panel
that mounts open, so a panel on screen from the start needs no
`preventDefault()` to keep the page's focus. `onCloseAutoFocus` works the same
way at the close. Focus the product has already moved out of the panel — to
the details of a place picked from the thread — is never taken back, and the
callback is not called.

Close is drawn at 36px, as the header was drawn round it, with a 44px target.

## A spoken conversation

Where the product has a voice model, the visitor can talk to the assistant
and hear it answer aloud. `AIInputBar` draws the microphone when the product
passes `onVoiceStart`, and says what the conversation is doing; its page has
the states and the wiring. Where there is no voice model, as in an App Clip,
leave `onVoiceStart` out and there is no microphone. The panel itself needs
nothing new, and moves focus exactly as above.

The thread still shows what was said, from the voice model's transcripts, as
`UserMessage` and `AIMessage` turns. Two things are the product's to get
right:

- **One voice at a time.** The thread is a polite live region, so a screen
  reader reads each turn as it arrives — while the assistant is already
  saying it aloud, and the two talk over each other. Turn the thread's
  announcements off while the conversation is live, and back on when it
  ends. The microphone's own region still says whose turn it is.

  ```tsx
  const live = ["connecting", "listening", "speaking"].includes(voiceState);

  <AIMessageList aria-live={live ? "off" : "polite"}>{turns}</AIMessageList>;
  ```

- **Closing ends it.** A panel closed while the conversation is live ends
  the conversation too — in `onClose`, as `onVoiceEnd` does. A microphone
  must never stay open behind a panel the visitor has closed.
