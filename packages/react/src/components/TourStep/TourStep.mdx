# TourStep

One step of a product tour (GAP-188, decision 116): a card beside the control the step points at,
which Backdrop's cut-out lights while the rest of the page is dimmed. It carries the tour's name
and its position ("Step 2 of 5"), a title, any body, a hint, the live status of what completes the
step ("Go ahead and open a site to proceed to the next step."), the progress as Stepper's dots,
Back, Next and Close. It is the MAP-64 boards' card in Kozmos's tokens: Popover's surface, the
Control corner, 16 inside, a 4 top edge of the theme fill, 340 wide.

TourStep is one step, and it is controlled: which step shows, and pausing and resuming the tour,
are the product's. On the docs page each example starts shut; its button opens it.

## A tour

Keep one TourStep and change its props as the tour moves on: `step`, `title`, the body, and
`target`. The cut-out glides to the next target, and the card is drawn anew for each `step`, so a
reader hears the new step as a dialog opening, and focus is on its Next. Pass `onBack` from the
second step on, and `nextLabel="Finish"` on the last; `onNext` on the last step is where the tour
ends. A step past `stepCount` draws every dot done, for a closing card, with `positionLabel="Done"`.

<Canvas of={TourStepStories.Step} />

## Where it sits

`target` is an element, a ref to one, a virtual element (anything with `getBoundingClientRect()`,
such as a point on a map) or a rect in the viewport's coordinates. The card sits 12 past the
cut-out's ring on its `side` (`"bottom"` by default), at `align` along it (`"center"`), flips to
the other side where there is no room, and keeps 16 from the window's edges. It is read every
frame, as Backdrop reads its target, so the card and the cut-out move together, whatever moves the
target, and a ref filled after the step opens is found.

With no target, or one that is not on the page (a ref to nothing, an element with no box), the
whole page is dimmed and the card places itself in the middle of the window: the boards' "Step
can’t be pointed to".

## The body

`children` takes any content: the boards put a row of Tags in it and a Table. `hint` is a tip
under it, on the themed surface, and `instruction` is what completes the step, beside a pulsing
marker. The marker holds still when motion is reduced, by the system or by the design config.

<Canvas of={TourStepStories.WithContent} />

## A single hint (coachmark)

With no `stepCount`, no `onBack` and no `onNext`, TourStep is a coachmark (GAP-157): a title and a
body beside the control it teaches, with no position and no dots, closed by its button and by
Escape. It takes focus on itself when it opens, so it is read out. Name its button with
`closeLabel`, "Dismiss" for example. For an action as well, pass `onNext` with `nextLabel="Got it"`.
There is no separate Coachmark part.

<Canvas of={TourStepStories.Coachmark} />

## Pressing outside, and closing

Escape and Close call `onClose`. A press on the dim or on the lit target does not: the dim and the
target are part of the tour, and the target takes the press through the cut-out, as "Go ahead and
open a site" asks. `onInteractOutside` is called on each such press, and when focus moves outside
the card; to close on one, call `onClose` from there. `open={false}` lets the step leave as a
popover does; the product may also stop rendering it.

## Stacking

The card is drawn in the provider's portal with its Backdrop, one above it whatever the order the
two arrive in, so a backdrop drawn anew never covers the card. A popover opened while the step is
open, from the body or by the lit target (a field's list, a tooltip), opens above both.

## Accessibility

TourStep is a non-modal dialog named by its title and described by its tour, its position, its
body, its hint and its status, so a reader hears "Open a site, dialog, How Pointr Cloud is
organised, Step 2 of 5, …" when it opens. It takes focus when it opens, on Next, or on itself when
it has no Next, whether a press opened it or the page did. The page stays reachable: Tab leaves the
card for the lit target, and the target keeps its own role. The status is a polite live region,
mounted with each step, so a status that arrives or changes while the step is open is read out.
The dots are drawn for the eye only, since the position is in words.

When the step closes with focus lost (Escape, Close, Finish), `PopoverContent` gives focus back
(GAP-190): to what had focus as the step opened, such as the button that started the tour, or,
when that is gone or the step opened with focus on the previous step's Next, to the step's target.
A virtual element or a rect has nothing to take it. A step's own Next keeps focus as the next step
opens.

The labels have English defaults (decision 100): `backLabel` "Back", `nextLabel` "Next",
`closeLabel` "Close" and `positionLabel` "Step {step} of {count}", a template or a function of the
step and the count.

Right to left, the card, its sections and the dots mirror, as the provider's direction says. At
compact density (Pointr Cloud's), Close is 32 and Back and Next 40.

import * as React from "react";
import { Button, TourStep } from "@kozmos-ds/react";

export function SitesTour() {
  const site = React.useRef<HTMLButtonElement>(null);
  const [step, setStep] = React.useState<number | null>(1);
  return (
    <>
      <Button ref={site} variant="outline">
        Healthcare Center
      </Button>
      <TourStep
        open={step !== null}
        target={site}
        tourLabel="How Pointr Cloud is organised"
        step={step ?? 1}
        stepCount={5}
        title="Open a site"
        instruction="Go ahead and open a site to proceed to the next step."
        onBack={step && step > 1 ? () => setStep(step - 1) : undefined}
        onNext={() => setStep((current) => (current ?? 1) + 1)}
        onClose={() => setStep(null)}
      >
        Each site in this client is listed here. Open one to see its buildings.
      </TourStep>
    </>
  );
}
    `}
