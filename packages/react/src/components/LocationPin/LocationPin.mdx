# LocationPin

The SDK's map result markers (decision 71): a result's number in a pill, a featured place's logo in a
tile, and the green pin for a place whose details are open. In a product these markers come from the
map style's sprite sheet, and a featured place's tile from the featured sprite Pointr's back end makes
with its logo. LocationPin draws the same markers for prototypes and boards (decision 81). The SDK host
owns geographic placement and selection state.

## A result

A result's number sits in a pill on a pointer, and the pointer's tip is the point it marks. The pill is
32 tall, as wide as its number and 8 more each side, 32 at least. At rest it is theme 600 on the
background; selected, it fills with theme 600 under foreground/1000, and doesn't grow. Its card's number
tab fills with it (decision 55). Without a number it shows a dot.

- **Contrast:** theme 600 on background/0 reads 6.24:1 in light and 6.17:1 in dark, and so does
  foreground/1000 on theme 600.
- **Selected is theme 600, not the theme fill** (theme 500, decision 59): it is the SDK's sprite.

## A featured place

A `featured` place is its logo in a tile: 44 square, 52 when selected, with a 3 rim in foreground/0
(black in light, white in dark) and corners of 16, on a dot whose centre is the point. Pass the logo,
normally an image, as `markerContent`; it fits inside the rim and is never cropped. The tile shows no
number, no star and no accent colour (decision 81). Its accessible name adds `featuredLabel` ("Featured"
unless you pass it translated) to `label`.

## A place on its own

`place` is the marker for a place whose details are open, the only one on the map: a green teardrop of
40 (emotional success 800, `#197F4C` in light, `#A0ECC6` in dark) round a disc, on a dot whose centre
is the point. It wins over `featured` and `number`.

## Its name

`label` is the marker's whole accessible name, as your product writes it, the number included ("Result 3,
Burger King"): word order and translation are the product's. LocationPin adds nothing to it except
`featuredLabel` on a featured tile, which shows no words of its own. The same holds on iOS and Android.

## Pressing and opening

A pin with `onClick` or `onOpen` is a button with a target 44 square at least. A double tap opens the
place's details through `onOpen` (decision 71) rather than zooming the map: the first tap is the
ordinary press, and the second calls `onOpen` once and isn't passed on as another. It is the same gate as
`POIResultList.onOpen`.

- **By keyboard,** Enter opens a pin that is already the current one (selected, or a place) or whose only
  action is `onOpen`. Otherwise Enter, and Space always, is the ordinary press: the first Enter selects a
  result, the second opens it. A held Enter opens once.
- **Its description** says so while Enter opens it: `openHint`, "Press Enter to open details" unless you
  pass it translated. It joins any `aria-describedby` you pass.
- **On iOS and Android,** VoiceOver and TalkBack get an Open action.

## On a map

- **A second tap stays on the pin.** MapLibre zooms on a second tap within 500 ms and cancels that tap's
  click, so a pin's details would never open. With `onOpen`, the second touch tap, and its `dblclick`,
  stop at the pin; a first tap, and a pan that starts on it, still reach the map. It holds for a marker
  rendered in a portal too (measured with MapLibre GL 4.7.1). A second tap after the double-tap time (300
  ms) but within MapLibre's 500 is an ordinary press, and the map doesn't zoom.
- **A press is a click on the map too.** MapLibre fires the map's `click` for a click on a marker (its own
  `Marker` relies on it), so a `click` handler that clears the selection would undo the pin's press. Skip a
  click inside a pin: `if (event.originalEvent.target.closest(".kozmos-location-pin")) return;`.
- **A pin nothing presses lets the map have it:** with no `onClick` or `onOpen`, or disabled, a press or a
  pan starting on it reaches the map.
- **`left` and `right` labels are the map's,** in either direction, as MapLibre places a symbol's text.
  Place a pin by its physical point. On iOS use `.kozmosLocationPinAt(point)`, which keeps the point in
  right-to-left layouts, where `.position` mirrors it; on Android, `Modifier.kozmosLocationPinAt { point }`,
  which places it absolutely.

## Results on another level

Mark only the results on the level in view; the level switcher counts the rest (`showResultCounts`).
LocationPin no longer has an off-floor state (decision 80).

## Upgrading from 0.11.0

- **`variant`, `size` and `tint`** are gone: the SDK's markers have one look. Remove them.
- **`offFloor`** is gone: don't mark other-level results.
- **Anchoring:** a pin is still placed by a physical point. A result's anchor is its pointer's tip, at
  its foot, as before; a featured tile's and a place's is their dot's centre, 3 above their foot.
